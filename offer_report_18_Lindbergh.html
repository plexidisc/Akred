Key Insights
◆ The gap between the highest offer price (Offer 2 at $2,220,000) and the recommended offer (Offer 1 at $2,195,000) is only $15,000 — a difference that is almost certainly eliminated by the risk premium of Offer 2's loan contingency and the cost of a potential financing fall-through.
◆ Offer 3's FHA financing is a meaningful flag on a 1930-built property. FHA appraisers are required to flag health and safety deficiencies in older homes. If the appraiser requires repairs as a condition of the loan, the seller may be forced to complete them or renegotiate the price — adding both cost and delay.
◆ Offer 3's escalation clause to $2,250,000 is only relevant if Offer 1 or Offer 2 can be used to trigger it. In practice, a seller's agent can present Offer 1 as the competing offer, potentially pushing Offer 3 to its ceiling — though this requires careful legal handling and full disclosure to all parties.
◆ The 21-day close on Offer 1 is unusually fast for this market and represents a meaningful benefit if the sellers need to free up capital or have already committed to a purchase elsewhere. In San Mateo's current market, time-certainty is often worth more than the marginal price difference between competing offers.
