The six biggest concerns

These are the items most likely to affect value, financing, insurance or how the home can be used.

High

1. Limited seller knowledge, sold as is

  • The seller is a successor trustee who never lived in the home, so she is exempt from the standard Transfer Disclosure Statement.
  • Only a short exempt seller form was provided. Nine solar questions were left unanswered for the same reason.
  • The home inspection was ordered by the seller's family, not the buyer.

What to do: get the buyer's own general inspection and rely on it more than usual.

High

2. Bedroom count and square footage may not match permits

  • Public record: 2 bedrooms, 2 baths, 2,096 sq ft. Listing: 3 bedrooms, 2.5 baths, 2,738 sq ft, measured from a 3D scan.
  • The 1963 certificate of occupancy describes a one story, 5 room home. City records show a permitted downstairs bath (1965) and a permitted addition (1971, signed off 1973).
  • The 2026 window permit refers to the downstairs rooms as both "utility room" and "bedroom." That downstairs bedroom had no working heat at inspection.
  • The records do not explain the roughly 640 sq ft difference.

What to do: request the full permit history from the City of Oakland and confirm whether the third bedroom is legal. Base value on permitted space unless confirmed.

High

3. The shared driveway parcel and a new neighbor sewer line

  • The sale includes a separate driveway parcel. Three neighbors hold recorded roadway easements over it, and the city holds sewer and storm drain easements.
  • No driveway maintenance agreement appears in the packet.
  • In summer 2026 the neighbor at 4096 39th Avenue installed a sewer line under the driveway without the seller's permission. The seller intends to grant that neighbor an easement.
  • The natural hazard report was researched for the house parcel only.

What to do: have a real estate attorney review the proposed easement before it is signed, and ask whether any maintenance or cost sharing agreement exists.

High

4. New roof, but leak damage remains

  • A new roof was installed in 2026 (reroof certificate closed March 30, 2026). The solar panels were removed and reinstalled.
  • The September pest report still found fungus damage to roof sheathing in the hallway from roof leaks, damaged roof beam ends and fascia.
  • Debris on the roof and leaf guards limited the home inspector's view.

What to do: ask for the roofing contract and warranty, and have a licensed roofer inspect the sheathing and the panel reinstallation.

High

5. Water getting in, in many places

  • Past water in the crawl space, utility room, garage and basement areas; standing water marks in low crawl space areas.
  • Fungus damage to framing, subfloor and joists under the hallway tile floor (no bid; referred to a contractor).
  • Stains at windows, doors, walls and ceilings; water appears to enter under the garage door.
  • Downspouts drain at the foundation and the rear grade slopes toward the house.
  • The whole interior and exterior were repainted in 2026. The pest inspector noted that fresh paint can hide past stains.

What to do: get a drainage contractor's evaluation and a bid for the hallway floor repair.

High

6. Steep hillside in an earthquake fault zone

  • The property is inside a state Alquist Priolo earthquake fault zone and a city seismic hazard zone with expansive soils. Future additions may require a fault study.
  • Some retaining walls lean or have loose blocks and may be taller than allowed without engineered plans. One 1991 wall was engineered; it is not clear which one.
  • The seller's engineer found the foundation serviceable but saw no significant seismic strengthening and recommends it.
  • The home inspector recommends a soils engineer for slope stability.

What to do: hire a geotechnical (soils) engineer and get earthquake insurance quotes early.

Permits and legal items

From the City of Oakland records and the preliminary title report.

Medium

Second kitchen

  • A 1974 city letter allowed a second kitchen only under a two year permit that had to be renewed by new application.
  • No renewal was found. The wet bar with a gas cooktop is most likely that kitchen.

Why it matters: rental or separate unit use may not be allowed.

Medium

2026 work without permits

  • No permits found for the rebuilt deck by the garage, downstairs wiring and lighting, or the new wall heater.
  • A permit for repairing the utility room floor was voided.
  • The window permit status is unclear; confirm the final inspection.
Note

Taxes and title

  • 2025 to 2026 taxes were $6,286 on a low assessed value. Expect a large increase at the new purchase price.
  • East Bay Regional Park Mello Roos tax of about $12 a year; about $2,125 a year in other special taxes.
  • Title requires a trust certification from the trustee.
Note

Fire zone

  • The hazard report says the home is not in a high or very high fire hazard zone.
  • Fire maps have been updated in recent years, and insurers use their own maps.

What to do: confirm with the current CAL FIRE map and an insurance quote.

Safety items

Medium
  • Missing handrails and guardrails on several stairs and at a retaining wall drop near the laundry door.
  • Decks lack lateral connectors to the house; some stair stringers are not properly supported; one guardrail is loose.
  • Railing openings wider than 4 inches and climbable rails, a concern with young children.
  • Garage fire separation has gaps; the door closer does not work well.
  • Doors that open over steps, and windows that swing out over walkways.
  • Rodent activity, including damage to the overhead service wires and the solar inverter connection.

Smaller notes

Note
  • The engineer's letter is addressed to "39th Street." The home inspector could not see the framing under the cantilevered addition and saw signs of past movement; the engineer, visiting earlier, found the added framing adequate.
  • Solar is owned (7.56 kW, Tesla, with a 27 kWh battery permitted in 2019). The seller does not know whether it has net metering, which credits power sent back to the grid.
  • The seller disclosed a death on the property within the past three years.
  • The buyer is responsible for bringing any older plumbing fixtures up to water saving standards.
  • The sewer lateral certificate (valid to 2033) and sidewalk certificate are in place.
  • The home was built before 1978 and was fully repainted in 2026. The packet has no lead paint testing.

Recommended next steps

  1. Order the buyer's own general home inspection, and walk through it with the inspector.
  2. Request the City of Oakland permit history and confirm the legal bedroom count and square footage.
  3. Have a real estate attorney review the driveway easements and the proposed neighbor sewer easement.
  4. Get a roofer, a drainage contractor, a geotechnical engineer and an electrician to inspect and bid.
  5. Get homeowners, earthquake and fire insurance quotes before removing contingencies.
  6. Ask the seller for the roofing contract and warranty, the solar documents, and proof that the 2026 window permit is closed.
